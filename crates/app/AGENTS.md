# crates/app

GPUI desktop app (`working`). One main window plus optional Meet call windows (macOS and Windows).

## Conventions

- `Store` is the one entity that holds core state and the lists views render. Views own presentation state and send `Command`s; they do not talk HTTP.
- The Mentions and Starred buttons (a sidebar row, a Chats header button, a rail pane, a row of the IRC buffer list) follow `Settings::show_mentions` and `Settings::show_starred`, copied into the `Store` by `sync_settings` so the sidebar items and rail tabs rebuild. A switch hides only the button: an open Mentions or Starred view stays open, and the other ways in keep working.
- The IRC layout (`Layout::Irc`) is the Messages list drawn another way: `conv_list.rs` shows the same `Store::list` as one monospaced line a conversation, grouped by kind (`Kind::buffer_rank`), under a one-line header (the filter in use, whose menu holds the filters, Unread only, and the two orders, then New chat); the views (Mentions, Starred, Calendar, Meet, Mail) are the first rows of that list, so its row indexes are the view rows and then `Store::list`'s. `conversation.rs` adds the one-line title bar and, for spaces, the nick list from `Command::LoadMembers`. The IRC message style (`MessageStyle::Irc`, `message::irc_row`) is separate from the layout and works in any of them; it reuses the builders of the other styles for everything under the text.
- One unread filter serves every layout: `Store::unread_only` (the Unread switch of Home and the Rail, the toggle at the right of the Messages chips, Unread only in the IRC filter menu) narrows whatever `Store::pane` lists, and picking a pane never turns it off. `Store::pane` always fits the layout on screen (`Pane::fits`: a section, Mentions, and Starred are panes of the Rail only; `sync_settings` goes back to All when a layout change leaves one). Every filter says where the unread are: a count on each Rail tab, on each Google Chat section header (`Store::section_unread`), on the Messages toggle and in the IRC menu, and a dot on a chip (`Store::pane_unread`). A list column narrower than `TITLE_MIN_WIDTH` (`conv_list::is_narrow`) is drawn for its width: the Messages header shows IRC's one-line filter in place of the chips and the two buttons, in the interface font, so it never wraps; the Rail's list header keeps its title beside the two small buttons of the Messages chip row (`ConvList::filter_buttons`, without the count, which the Rail's tabs carry) in place of its Sort button and labeled switch; and the rows of the Messages and Rail lists are stacked (`RowMarks::stacked`: the name beside the time, the preview beside the marks) so names keep their room. The one-line filter's menu opens under the line, whose bounds a `canvas` child records as it is painted; the other menus open at the pointer.
- What a list shows as unread is `store::shows_unread`, never `Group::unread` read directly: a muted conversation reads as unread (bold, marked, counted, kept by the Unread filter) only once it mentions the user, as the web app does. Rows end with `ui::unread_mark`, a slot of one size whether it holds the "@" of a mention (`store::shows_mention`, from `Group::mentioned`), the unread dot, or nothing, and a muted conversation carries `ui::muted_mark` in every layout. Muted conversations come last where conversations are grouped, as the web app lists them: in a Google Chat section after its pinned and its other conversations (`store::section_order`), in the IRC list at the end of their kind (`store::list_key`); the Messages and Rail lists and Home stay by recency. The Google Chat sidebar keeps the rows past a section's Show all cut that show as unread or are open (`store::sidebar_rows`, plain code with tests). Each list scrolls to the row of the view that is asked for, whatever asked, also when that view is already on screen (`Store::view_asks` counts every `open`, `go_home`, and `open_*`): the sidebar to a conversation or to Home, Mentions, Starred, Calendar, Meet, or Mail (`sidebar::view_row`), the IRC list to a conversation or one of its view rows (`conv_list::open_row`), the Messages and Rail lists to a conversation.
- `Settings::list_window` holds stale spaces and group chats back from `Store::list` (`store::is_older`, plain code with tests): they wait behind the list's Show older row, and never while a search query is typed. `Store::list` is a `Listing`, so rows, indexes and keyboard stepping go through it.
- A thread opens beside the conversation or as a pop-up (`Settings::thread_view`). The window root draws the pop-up (`RootView::thread_popup`) so it centers on the window and sits under the menus and pickers; `ConversationView::keyed` puts the composers' keys on it, since it is outside the conversation's element. Modals that follow the window's size use `conversation::modal_size`.
- Widgets come from gpui-kit (`use gpui_kit::*;` is the kit prelude). Colors come from `theme.rs` through `rgb()`; no literal colors in other files.
- The interface font and the monospace font are `Settings::font` and `Settings::mono_font` (family names; absent is System, and a family no longer installed reads as System, because GPUI panics laying out a missing family). `settings::show_theme` keeps the resolved families in `theme::set_fonts` and on the kit theme's `font_family` / `mono_font_family`. Only a change of appearance, theme, or font (`Settings::same_look`) puts them on the windows again and restyles every window; other setting changes do not, and the settings file is written off the UI thread. The main window and the Meet window roots set `theme::sans()`, and code spans and blocks use `theme::mono()`. System is `theme::SYSTEM_FONT` (`.SystemUIFont`, what GPUI uses with no family: the system font on macOS, Segoe UI on Windows; on Linux the first installed of `theme::SYSTEM_SANS`, named, because GPUI drops a font's fallbacks when it resolves `.SystemUIFont`) and `theme::SYSTEM_MONO`: on macOS `.AppleSystemUIFontMonospaced` (Core Text resolves it without SF Mono in Font Book), on Windows Consolas, on Linux the first installed of `theme::SYSTEM_MONOS` (GPUI matches family names exactly there, so fontconfig's `monospace` alias reads as missing). Never name a font family literally elsewhere.
- Emoji go through `emoji_font.rs`, which does nothing off Linux: `emoji_font::text` for labels that may hold emoji (avatars, reactions, the picker, previews), `emoji_font::runs` over the highlights and font overrides of `StyledText` message text, and `emoji_font::fallback` on a window root. On Linux they draw every emoji with the bundled `Working Emoji` font that `scripts/emoji-font.sh` builds into `assets/fonts/` ([docs/workarounds.md](../../docs/workarounds.md)). New text that can hold emoji uses them too.
- The Calendar view (`calendar.rs`: toolbar, side panel, keys; `calendar_grid.rs`: the six views; `event_card.rs`, `event_editor.rs`: the card and the editor, drawn by the window root as overlays) talks to the core only through the `Calendar*` commands and events held in `Store::calendar`. Its date and lane math is plain code in `calendar_layout.rs` with tests. Event and calendar colors are data (`theme::data_color`, `ink_on`), the one place colors come from outside the theme. Its button follows the layout: a sidebar row (Google Chat), a Chats header button (Messages), a rail button (Rail), a row of the buffer list (IRC); all dispatch `OpenCalendar` (`Cmd+2`). It is the Calendar feature (`Settings::calendar`, see Features).
- Features (Settings, Features, first in its sidebar): Calendar, Meet, and Mail are each on or off as a whole (`Settings::calendar`, `meet`, `mail`; `Settings::features` gives `settings::Features`). A feature that is on always shows its button. One that is off is gone everywhere: no button, Go menu entry, or number key (`Features::opens`; `RootView::open_hotkey` and the Assign shortcut submenu ignore its key), its view is never shown (`store::shows`: `Store::open_*` refuse it, Back and Forward skip it, `Store::sync_settings` goes Home when it goes off on screen), no notifications (Mail: `StopMail` and no Dock count; Calendar: `WatchEvents(false)`, no reminders; Meet: no call window, `call::open` uses the browser), no MCP server (`Settings::mcp_servers` passes a server, and its write flag, only while its feature and switch are on; `keep_mcp` follows that), and no core traffic of its own (the views load only while on screen). In Settings its other rows and its MCP group are disabled with "<Feature> is off in Settings, Features." (`settings_view::feature_off`). Meet on with Calendar off still reads events with a Meet link for the Meet view; its "Schedule in Google Calendar" entry is gone then. "View buttons" in Layout keeps only Mentions and Starred.
- Message text is drawn with `StyledText`, which cannot be selected. A click on a text bubble (press and release on it, its quote included) opens `text_panel.rs`: a floating panel at the pointer with the raw text in the kit's read-only `Textarea` (select, Cmd+A, Cmd+C, Copy all), drawn by the window root and opened through `text_panel::show`. A link or mention in the text stops the click so it only opens its target, and so does the read-receipt overlay. It closes on Escape (handled first in `RootView::on_escape`), on a press outside, and when the view or conversation changes; while it is open `modal_open` is true. Drop it once the kit's selectable text can carry links.
- The Meet view (`meet.rs`: toolbar, tabs, keys; `meet_body.rs`: the Meetings and Calls tabs and the card with a new meeting's link) talks to the core only through `CreateMeeting`, `StartMeet`, `CreateChat`, and `LoadEvents`, and holds its state in `Store::meet`. The events of its day are a range of their own, apart from `Store::calendar`'s, so each view takes only the answers for its range. A call to someone without a chat makes the chat first: `OpenChat` then posts the meeting in it instead of opening it, and the call is done once the meeting card shows there. What Join accepts, the week strip, and which meetings a day lists are plain code in `meet_logic.rs` with tests. Its button follows the layout: a sidebar row (Google Chat), a Chats header button (Messages), a rail button (Rail), a row of the buffer list (IRC); all dispatch `OpenMeet` (`Cmd+3`). It is the Meet feature (`Settings::meet`).
- The Mail view (`mail.rs`: toolbar, list, reading pane, keys; `mail_logic.rs`: names, sizes, the split, the selection's steps, reply recipients, the selection after an archive, and a new email's addresses, plain code with tests) is Gmail: it talks to the core only through `LoadMail`, `OpenMail`, `StopMail`, and the writes `MarkMail`, `ArchiveMail`, `TrashMail`, `ReplyMail`, and `ComposeMail` (`mail_write.rs`, `mail_compose.rs`), and holds what they answer in `Store::mail`. The core applies a write to the lists and the open thread at once and answers `MailWritten`, so the view keeps no state of a write but the reply drafts and one unsent new email. The reading pane sits beside the list when `root::panel_width` leaves room (`mail_logic::splits`), else in its place; New email uses that same place. Its keys are bound in `keys.rs` under the "Mail" context (arrows, Enter, Escape); its search field's "Input" context keeps those keys while it has the keyboard, and its Escape reaches `RootView::on_escape`, which asks `MailView::dismiss`. A file opens through `viewer::open_attachment` (`mail_logic::attachment`), so the viewer reads it through the core. The Mail feature (`Settings::mail`) is whether Gmail is read at all. On, `Store::start_mail` asks for the inbox once the connection is up, which has the core watch it for the unread count (also on the Dock number) and new mail. Off, `Store::stop_mail` sends `StopMail`, clears `Store::mail`, and leaves the view, and answers still on their way are dropped. Its button follows the layout with the unread inbox count (a sidebar row, a Chats header button, a rail button, a row of the IRC buffer list); all dispatch `OpenMail` (`Cmd+4`).
- Mail writes (`mail_write.rs`, `mail_compose.rs`): a thread's emails reaching the screen with the window in front mark it read (`Event::MailMessages` in `Store::apply`), as a conversation opened is. Mark as unread and, in the Inbox only, Archive sit on the reading pane's head and the selected row; mark as unread goes back to the list; archive closes the reading pane and selects the thread after it (`mail_logic::after_removal`) without opening it, since opening marks read (Enter opens it), and shows an Undo bar under the list for 8 seconds or until the next action. Move to Trash sits only on the reading pane's head and moves the open thread to Gmail's Trash, then opens the thread after it (before it at the end), which marks that one read; the pane closes only when the list has no other. Escape still closes the pane without moving the thread; the narrow pane also has a back arrow. The store matches each `MailWritten` to the oldest write sent (the core runs them one at a time, in order) as `MailSent::Reply` or `MailSent::Compose`. Reply and Reply all (only when `mail_logic::adds_people`) open a box under the last email, addressed by `gchat_core::reply_recipients`; New email (`c`, toolbar) opens a form with To and Cc chips (`mail_logic::take_addresses`, `address_ok`, `rank_addresses`), a subject, and a body. Send (`secondary-enter`) is off without a recipient, with an invalid one, with both subject and body empty, or while a send is going; the form or box closes on `MailWritten(Ok)` (`Store::take_sent`) and keeps its text and shows the error on `Err`. One unsent new email is kept while the app runs when the form is left. Drafts stay per thread in the view while the app runs. Rewrite on a reply or a new-email body uses `rewrite::Kind::Mail` and the same button as Chat. The one-letter keys `c`, `u`, `e`, `r`, `a` are the view's own key handler, live only while the view itself has the keyboard: a binding in the Mail context would also fire while a text field inside it has the keyboard.
- Mail lists (`mail_lists.rs`, plain code with tests that replay the core's answers in the orders they come): each mailbox's list moves between asked (`loading`), answered (`loaded`), and failed (its own `failed`, shown in its place with Retry); a `MailThreads` is stored whatever else is pending, and a `MailFailed` fails only the lists on their way (else it is a thread's, shown in the reading pane). `MailView::sync` asks again for the mailbox on screen whenever it has nothing, nothing on its way, and no failure (`MailLists::needs_asking`). `Store::apply` traces each mail event at debug level with its kind, the mailbox kind, and counts, never text.
- An email shown whole renders its HTML in a webview of its own (`mail_body.rs`, built only through `webview::build`), serving the page `mail_page::page` makes (plain code with tests) under the `mailbody` protocol: a Content-Security-Policy that runs nothing of the email's (no scripts, frames, forms) and lets only inline styles and images through, the theme's background, text, and link colors by default, and remote images rewritten to `/image?u=…`, which the app fetches through `Core::fetch`. Links leave for the browser (navigation and new-window handlers, `webview::is_under` for the page's own address on each OS); our `mail_page::SCRIPT` reports the email's height (the card sizes to it), the wheel (the reading pane scrolls), and Escape. Webviews are native views over GPUI's: `RootView::covers_panel` hides them while a window overlay is open (`MailView::set_covered`), and they are dropped when their email goes back to one line, another thread opens, the theme changes, or Mail leaves the screen. On Windows gpui-webview cannot clip a webview to the scrolling pane (no window composition), so a page partly scrolled out draws past the pane there.
- An email card whose email has HTML carries a background switch (the `Contrast` icon in its head, accent while on): emails set their own text colors expecting white, so one can be unreadable on a dark theme. A click toggles `Settings::mail_other_background`, saved in `settings.json`, which applies to every email: while it is on, `MailView::sync_bodies` makes every page with `mail_page::Colors::other` of the theme in use (a light page under a dark theme, a dark one under a light theme; colors other than the pages were made with make them all again, so a click or a theme change re-renders every email shown) and `mail_body::other_background` puts each page in a frame of that color. MailView observes `Settings` to redraw.
- Mail notifications: `Event::MailIncoming` posts through `notify::post` with the key `mail/<thread id>` (title the sender, subtitle the subject, body the snippet), unless `Settings::mail_notify` is off, `status::quiet` holds, or the window is in front on Mail. A click reaches `Store::open_key`, which opens Mail on that thread, read once its emails show. The Dock number (`Store::count_badge`) adds `MailData::unread` while the Mail feature (`Settings::mail`) is on.
- MCP servers: one per product, each its own switch in Settings, Agents (`Settings::mcp_chat`, `mcp_mail`, `mcp_calendar`, `mcp_meet`; the write switches `mcp_send`, `mcp_mail_write`, and `mcp_calendar_write` are disabled while their server is off), at `http://127.0.0.1:<mcp_port>/mcp/<chat|mail|calendar|meet>`. `Settings::mcp_servers` says what runs: a server only while its switch and its feature are on, a write flag only while its server runs. `Settings::mcp_config` is `None` while none runs; `keep_mcp` restarts the core's servers when what runs, the port, or the token changes. The section shows each server's link and Claude Code command (`working-<server>`), and the status, the shared token, and Replace once.
- Avatars and attachment thumbnails load through `Images` (core fetch, background decode). Views do not decode bytes on the UI thread or use GPUI's default asset loader for those URLs.
- Read receipts: `Store::receipts` holds the `Event::ReadReceipts` of the open conversation only. An event for it replaces them and only a change reaches `ConversationView::set_readers`, which keeps none while the conversation has receipts off; the next view that is not that conversation drops them, because the core sends them again whenever one opens. Each person's avatar goes on the newest message of the main list, and separately of the thread panel's list, sent at or before their read time (`message::Readers`, over `receipt_logic::place`, plain code with tests: system lines, messages still going out, and failed ones are never the last read, and a person's own messages count). The list is virtualized and measures a row once, so receipts take no part in a row's layout. Every row, read or not, puts the part the avatars sit on (the text bubble, or else the last attachment, or else the last card; never the reactions or the thread footer) in `read_mark`: a row of the part, in a column of its own that keeps the width it would have in the message's column, and a holder (`flex_1`, zero basis) that takes only the room beside it, which is why the message's column fills its row (`flex_1`). The avatars (`readers_group`: 16 px with a 1 px ring in the page color, at most four overlapping and then `+N` in muted text, a tooltip that names them, a click that stops there) lie on an absolutely placed track over the holder, on the part's bottom edge, from 4 px inside the part's bottom corner (bottom-right; bottom-left of the user's own right-aligned bubbles) to 2 px short of the row's edge: beside the part, growing outward, while they fit, else against the track's far end, over the corner. Nothing between the track and the list may clip (`overflow_hidden`).
- The New line: a conversation opened unread shows a rule labeled "New" (or "New · mentions you") above the first message of someone else sent after its read time, which `ConversationView` keeps from the `Group` it is made with (`read_at_open`), since opening marks it read. Where it goes is `receipt_logic::new_line`, plain code with tests: the user's own messages move the read time on, a reply the main list does not show stands for its thread's row, and a conversation unread with nothing past its read time (marked unread by hand) gets the line above the newest message of someone else, only once the core's latest messages have come. The main list opens scrolled to it. Once placed it is kept by message id and does not move as messages come; the thread panel places its own when it opens; sending a message takes both away. The row under it starts a run, and a list measures that row again when the line comes or goes (`MessageList::set_new_line`).
- New replies and messages below the fold: a thread starter's footer in the main list says how many of its replies are new (`3 replies · 2 new`, `ThreadInfo::new`, counted by `receipt_logic::new_replies`, plain code with tests): those of someone else sent after the latest of the conversation's own time (`read_at_open`, else `caught_up`: the open or the user's last send), the last time that thread's panel was on screen (`threads_seen`), and the user's own newest reply there; the thread whose panel is open counts none. A message of someone else that arrives at the end while the main list is scrolled up from it (`MessageList::at_end`, asked before the rows change) is counted in `LoadState::unseen`, and a pill over the bottom of the list says how many (`N new messages`); a click goes to the end, and reaching the end or sending clears it. `at_end` reads the list's scroll position and its last row's bounds, never `ListState::is_scrolled_to_end`, which answers `None` while rows above the ones drawn have no height, as in any long conversation. The pill, which is also Jump to latest around a search hit, lies over the list so it moves neither the list nor the composer. Each `MessageList` redraws every minute for the times its rows show.
- The main list's scroll handler reads nothing from its event: GPUI calls it while it holds the list's state, with the range the scroll started from. It defers `ConversationView::check_scroll`, which reads where the list landed (`MessageList::at_top`, `at_end`) and from that asks for older history at the top, for newer history at the end of a window around a search hit, and clears the pill at the end. A message's hover toolbar follows the row under the pointer: a row reports the pointer coming (`Act::Hover`) and going (`Act::Unhover`, also when the list scrolls the row from under a still pointer, which GPUI reports once the scroll rests), and the toolbar goes unless the pointer went onto the toolbar or onto another message.
- Every `cx.observe` / `cx.subscribe` / `cx.observe_global` handle is stored in a struct field; a dropped `Subscription` is a silent no-op.
- Meet runs Google's web app in a webview (`call.rs`, macOS and Windows): `WKWebView` with a Safari user agent on macOS, WebView2 with its own agent on Windows. `call.js` strips the page and drives the controls; the window chrome is ours. Session cookies go into the webview; rotated cookies are written back through `gchat_core::save_session_cookies`. On Windows every webview keeps its data in `gchat_core::webview_dir()`. Linux has no call window: `call::open` there opens the meeting in the default browser, because WebKitGTK as Fedora builds it has no WebRTC.
- The call's chat is Meet's own, drawn by the call window, and never goes through the core. `call.js` keeps Meet's chat panel open behind the tiles and reads it into `chat|` reports (JSON: each message's sender, time, and text in parts, a link part with its address; and whether the meeting lets the user write, `null` until Meet's panel is there). `sendChat` writes into Meet's field, presses its send button, and answers `sent|1` once Meet emptied the field or `sent|0` when it did not: the draft stays in the app's field until then. The data and its rules are plain code in `call_chat.rs` with tests; the panel beside the tiles, the unread count on the chat button, and the preview beside the controls are `CallView`'s. A message that comes while the call window is not the active one is a notification under the `meet-chat/<code>` key (quiet under do not disturb, `status::quiet`); the window root passes a clicked one to `call::show_chat`. `call.js` runs Meet's animation frames from a timer while the page is hidden, because WebKit stops them for a covered window and Meet draws new messages in one. Message text goes only to the panel and the notification, never to a log or `WORKING_TRACE`.
- The application menus are `root::menus()`, the one list of them: on macOS the system menu bar, and on Linux and Windows the main window's top bar, which has no menu bar. There the bar runs sidebar toggle (when the sidebar is on the left), mark, search field, status and sidebar toggle (when it is on the right), a menu button whose dropdown has each menu as a submenu with its shortcuts, then the kit's window buttons. The right-hand group reserves 150px there instead of macOS's 252px, the width of those three 34px buttons less, so the field stays centered and nothing leaves the window at its 800px minimum width.
- On macOS the app is the only screen-capture client in a call. `screen.rs` opens the macOS picker and captures with ScreenCaptureKit; `call.js` answers Meet's `getDisplayMedia` with a canvas stream of those frames. Never let the webview capture the screen alongside it: macOS gives a picker's answer to one capture client per bundle ID, and the other one's request then never settles. `screen.rs`, `permissions.rs`, and the Settings Permissions section are macOS only; on Windows Meet uses the webview's own screen sharing.
- Sign-in is an in-app webview (`welcome.rs`) that loads Google's account page, writes `cookies.json`, and sends `Command::Connect`. Cookie access for Meet and sign-in is the `session/` seam (`mod.rs`, then `macos.rs`, `linux.rs`, `windows.rs`): it goes to the platform cookie store directly, never through wry's `set_cookie`, which drops the leading dot of `.google.com`.
- Every webview (sign-in, Meet, the viewer) is made with `webview::build`, which hands it to a callback once it is there; never call `gpui_webview::WebView::build` directly. Initialization scripts and the first URL go to `webview::build`, not the builder: on Windows the build runs outside the app borrow and the scripts are added without wry's blocking wait ([docs/workarounds.md](../../docs/workarounds.md)).
- `crates/app/assets/logo.png` and `logo-light.png` are built by `just logo` from `logo/logo.svg`. Edit the SVG, then regenerate.
- Notifications are the `notify/` seam (`mod.rs`, then one file per OS). macOS: Notification Center and the Dock badge, only for the `.app` bundle (`just run`); a bare `cargo run -p working` binary has no notification center. Linux: freedesktop notifications over D-Bus (`zbus`) and the unread count as the launcher-entry signal on `working.desktop`; they are not grouped. Windows: toasts under the `AppUserModelID` `dev.felipefdl.working`, which shows only when a Start Menu shortcut carries it (the installer's), and no badge. Off macOS the sound is Default or None.
- The window root stacks its bottom-right cards (`updater::card`, and on Linux `desktop_entry::card`, the AppImage's apps-menu question) in one column; a new card joins that column. `desktop_entry.rs` keeps its decisions (the opt-outs, `Choice`, an entry it did not write) in plain functions whose tests use their own temp directories, never the real home or environment.
- Rewrite tools (`rewrite.rs`) run one headless job with no instruction files, skills, tools, MCP servers, hooks, plugins, or memory. Chat and Mail share `rewrite::run`, `rewrite::spawn`, and `rewrite::button`; `Kind::Chat` and `Kind::Mail` select the instruction. The draft goes to the CLI on stdin inside `<draft>` tags (`rewrite::tagged`), and every system prompt starts by saying so (`rewrite::DRAFT`): a CLI joins stdin with the prompt it is given, and without the tags a one-word draft came back with the instruction rewritten into it. While `Settings::rewrite_context` is on, the view also hands `spawn` the conversation's latest messages (`rewrite::earlier`: the Chat conversation or thread, or the email thread a reply answers; a new email has none), which go before the draft inside `<conversation>` tags, and the prompt asks for the rewrite in their language (`rewrite::AWARE`). The user's instructions stay last in the prompt so they win; a new prompt part goes before them. Before adding or changing one, meet [docs/rewrite-tools.md](../../docs/rewrite-tools.md); Grok is out until it has a bare mode.
- Rewrite's model and effort are per tool (`Settings::rewrite_choices`, `RewriteChoice`); `None` is Harness default and passes no flag (`rewrite::chosen`). Settings fills its Model picker from `rewrite::models`, which runs the CLI for Codex and Pi, so it is called off the main thread; `rewrite::efforts` says which efforts a model takes. What each tool lists and takes is in [docs/rewrite-tools.md](../../docs/rewrite-tools.md).
- The composers' formatting (the bar above a composer's text, shown by the toggle at its right, and `Cmd+B`, `Cmd+I`, `Shift+Cmd+X`, `Shift+Cmd+8`, Ctrl off macOS) writes Google Chat's own markup into the draft: `*bold*`, `_italic_`, `~strike~`, `` `code` ``, `- ` bullets, and three backticks around a code block. The kit's `Textarea` cannot show styled text, so the app sends the draft unchanged and the core turns the markers into formatting on send. What each format does to a text and a selection is plain code in `format_logic.rs` with tests; `conversation.rs` applies it as range edits (`set_selected_range`, then `replace`), never with `set_value` or `replace_all`, so chips stay and undo keeps its history. The kit applies one range per edit, so a format that changes two places (a pair of markers) takes one `Cmd+Z` for each. The keys are bound in the kit's `Input` context in `keys.rs` and act on the composer that has the keyboard, bar shown or not; Shift+8 arrives as the key `*` with the shift folded in, so that binding is `secondary-*`. `Settings::show_format_bar` shows the bar.
- Event reminders: `Event::EventReminder` posts through `notify::post` with the key `event/<calendar id>/<event id>` (title the event's, subtitle `calendar_layout::starts_in`, body its time and place or "Google Meet"), while `Settings::event_reminders` and the Calendar feature (`Settings::calendar`) are on and `status::quiet` does not hold. `Store::sync_event_watch` sends `Command::WatchEvents(true)` once the connection is up with both on, and `WatchEvents(false)` when either goes off. A click (`Store::open_key`) opens Calendar, which takes the event (`Store::take_reveal`) and shows its day and card. Mail's own changes: picking a mailbox, a label, or a search closes the thread open; the signed-in user's address reads "You" in the list (`mail_logic::senders_line`) and as their name in a card; an email wider than its card is scaled down to it by `mail_page::SCRIPT`, which reports the scaled height.
- Mail's label browser (`mail_labels.rs`): a control beside the mailbox switcher (hidden while `MailData::labels` is empty) names the label on screen by its own name, its whole path in the tooltip with the key, and opens a popover drawn by the Mail view under its toolbar: a filter field, then `mail_logic::label_rows` (the tree A to Z with foldable parents, or `Fuzzy` matches by whole path), each row with its color (`theme::data_color`), its name bold and `ui::count_badge` while it has unread threads. The arrows move the pick (`mail_logic::step`) from the filter field through the panel's key handler, Enter or a click picks, Escape or a press outside closes. Picking goes through `MailView::pick_mailbox` with `Mailbox::Label(id)`, so the search text goes and the open thread closes. `keys::MAIL_LABELS` (`secondary-l`, Mail context) opens and closes it. While it is open the email webviews hide: `MailView::apply_cover` at once, and `RootView::covers_panel` counts it too (the root observes the Mail view for that). Labels come from `Event::MailLabels` and go with the rest of `Store::mail` on `StopMail` and sign-out.
